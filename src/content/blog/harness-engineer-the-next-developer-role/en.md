It's quite likely.

Indeed, the better agentic development models and tools get, the more the developer's role shifts up a level.

Little by little, developers are leaving implementation details behind (where AI is already more relevant, and faster, than most developers), to move up to the level above: architecture, conventions, technical choices, business rules.

And when you barely write code by hand anymore (or not at all), there is ONE thing that acts as a guardrail for the models: the [Harness](/blog/ai-harness-guides-and-sensors/).

The Harness is everything around the AI model that makes an agent do what you want it to do. Instructions, tools, rules, tests, hooks, CI...

And watch out, this is nowhere near blind, uncontrolled [vibe-coding](/blog/the-part-time-senior-developer/).

It's the exact opposite.

## What a Harness Engineer has to do

The Harness Engineer has to:

- define a strict framework, know what they want and what they don't
- make as many rules as possible impossible to break
- spot drift as early as possible and fix the harness as they go
- deal with [technical debt](/blog/daily-rates-after-ai/) as fast as it shows up

## A whole new set of skills

They also have to master a whole new area of knowledge: context management, long sessions, multi-session tasks, orchestration, cost optimization...

... and on top of all that, they will very often be [the only developer on the codebase](/blog/zero-hand-written-code-experiment/). So full-stack skills, infra, even design or product.

## The final boss of development

In short, the Harness Engineer is to development what the final boss is to a video game. The ultimate senior, able to run a horde of agents without sacrificing quality.

My bet: within a few months, this job title will be trending on LinkedIn :) — one more symptom of the [ongoing shift in the developer market](/blog/the-developer-market-is-shifting/).
